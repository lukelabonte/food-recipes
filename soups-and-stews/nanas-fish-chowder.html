Nana's Fish Chowder

A soul-warming, old-fashioned New England-style fish chowder with tender cod, buttery potatoes, and a rich, creamy milk broth — simple comfort food at its finest.

Overview Ingredients Steps Tips Nutrition Weight
  • Prep 10 min
  • Cook 30 min
  • Total 40 min
  • Servings 4
  • Method Stovetop

Ingredients

  • 1 small onion, diced (~90 g)
    • Leek (white and light green parts only) — 1:1 by volume Milder, slightly sweeter flavor that blends beautifully into cream-based chowders; similar calorie level
    • Shallots — 1:1 by volume More delicate and slightly sweet; works seamlessly in a milk-based chowder without overpowering the fish
  • 1 cup butter, divided (½ cup for cooking, ½ cup added at the end) (~225 g)
    • Unsalted butter — 1:1 Allows better control of salt levels; same calorie level
    • Half butter, half olive oil — 1:1 total fat volume Reduces saturated fat; slightly changes richness but maintains good body in the broth; lower saturated fat
  • 2 cans (15 oz each) diced potatoes, drained (~850 g drained)
    • Fresh Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and diced — 1:1 by weight Better texture and flavor than canned; requires 15–20 extra minutes of simmering; same calorie level
    • Fresh russet potatoes, peeled and diced — 1:1 by weight Will break down more than Yukon Gold, creating a naturally thicker broth; same calorie level
  • Frozen cod fillets, thawed — approximately 1.5 lbs (680 g) (~680 g)
  • Water, enough to cover onion and potatoes — approximately 2 cups (~475 g)
    • Bottled clam juice diluted with water (50/50) — 1:1 by volume Adds deeper seafood flavor to the base broth; same calorie level
  • Whole milk, enough to cover after fish cooks — approximately 3 cups (~730 g)
    • 2% milk — 1:1 Slightly thinner broth but still creamy; lower calorie
    • Half-and-half — 1:1 Richer, more decadent broth with less risk of scalding; higher calorie
  • Salt and pepper, to taste — approximately ½ tsp salt and ¼ tsp black pepper (~3 g)

Steps

  1. Place the diced onion and ½ cup (1 stick) of butter into a large heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Allow the butter to melt and the onion to soften and become translucent, about 4–5 minutes. Do not brown the onion.
  2. Add the drained diced canned potatoes to the pot. Season generously with salt and pepper. Pour in just enough water to barely cover the onion and potatoes — approximately 2 cups. Bring the pot to a gentle simmer over medium heat.
  3. Cut the thawed cod fillets into large chunks, roughly 2–3 inches each. Gently lay the cod pieces on top of the simmering potato mixture. Do not stir — let the fish sit on top so it steams and poaches gently.
  4. Cover the pot and continue to simmer on medium-low heat for 10–15 minutes, until the fish turns completely white and flakes apart easily when pressed with a fork. The fish will naturally begin to break into the chowder.
  5. Pour in enough whole milk to fully cover the ingredients, approximately 3 cups. Add the remaining ½ cup of butter and adjust seasoning with additional salt and pepper to taste.
  6. Reduce the heat to low. Cook very gently, stirring frequently, until the chowder is fully heated through — about 8–10 minutes. Do not allow the milk to boil or scald, as this will cause it to curdle and lose its smooth, creamy texture. Serve immediately in warm bowls.

Tips

  • Don't Rush the Milk The most critical step in this recipe is heating the milk slowly over low heat. Scalded or boiled milk will curdle and create an unpleasant grainy texture. Keep the heat very low and stir constantly once the milk goes in.
  • Use Fresh Cod for Better Flavor While frozen cod works perfectly well (and is what Nana used), fresh cod fillets will yield a slightly cleaner, sweeter flavor. If using frozen, thaw overnight in the refrigerator for best texture.
  • Don't Stir the Fish While It Cooks Placing the cod on top and letting it steam undisturbed ensures it breaks apart naturally into large, tender flakes rather than turning mushy from being stirred around in the hot liquid.
  • Canned vs. Fresh Potatoes Canned diced potatoes are pre-cooked and very soft, which is why this chowder comes together so quickly. If substituting fresh potatoes, add them with the water and simmer for 15–20 minutes before adding the fish to ensure they are fully tender.
  • Chowder Gets Better the Next Day Like most chowders, this one tastes even richer after sitting overnight in the refrigerator. Reheat gently on very low heat, never boiling, and add a small splash of milk if it has thickened too much.
