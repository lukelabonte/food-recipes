Linda's Zucchini Bread

A warmly spiced, impossibly moist quick bread packed with fresh zucchini and toasted nuts — the kind of heirloom recipe that disappears from the counter before it has a chance to cool.

Overview Ingredients Steps Tips Nutrition Weight
  • Prep 20 min
  • Cook 60 min
  • Total 80 min
  • Servings 24
  • Method Oven

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs (150 g)
    • Flax eggs — 1:1 (1 tbsp ground flaxseed + 3 tbsp water per egg) Works well in quick breads; slightly denser texture, lower cholesterol, vegan-friendly
  • 2 cups granulated sugar (400 g)
    • Coconut sugar — 1:1 Adds a mild caramel note; slightly lower glycemic index but similar calorie level
    • Light brown sugar — 1:1 Adds a touch of molasses depth and keeps the crumb very moist
  • 1 cup vegetable oil (218 g)
    • Unsweetened applesauce — 1:1 Significantly lower calorie; produces a softer, slightly denser loaf with a mild fruity note
    • Melted coconut oil — 1:1 Similar calorie level; imparts a very subtle coconut flavor that complements the cinnamon
  • 1 tablespoon vanilla extract (13 g)
  • 2 cups grated zucchini, including seeds and peels (240 g)
  • 3 cups all-purpose flour (360 g)
    • Whole wheat flour — 1:1 Adds nuttiness and fiber; produces a slightly denser, heartier loaf
    • Half all-purpose, half whole wheat flour — 1:1 combined Good middle ground — slightly more fiber without sacrificing tenderness
  • 1 teaspoon salt (6 g)
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda (6 g)
  • 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon (8 g)
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder (2 g)
  • 1 cup chopped walnuts or pecans (120 g)
    • Pepitas (pumpkin seeds) — 1:1 Nut-free option; slightly lower calorie with a mild, toasty crunch
  • 1/2 cup chocolate chips (optional) (85 g)
    • Raisins or dried cranberries — 1:1 Adds chewy fruitiness instead of richness; lower fat but similar sugar content

Steps

  1. Preheat your oven to 325°F (165°C). Thoroughly grease and flour three medium (8×4-inch) loaf pans, tapping out any excess flour, and set aside.
  2. In a large mixing bowl, beat the eggs with an electric mixer on medium-high speed until light and fluffy, about 2 minutes.
  3. With the mixer running on low, slowly stream in the sugar, then increase speed to medium and beat well until the mixture is pale and slightly thickened, about 2 more minutes.
  4. Add the oil, vanilla extract, and grated zucchini to the egg mixture and stir until fully combined. The batter will look slightly curdled — that is normal.
  5. In a separate bowl, sift together the flour, salt, baking soda, ground cinnamon, and baking powder. Add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients and fold gently with a spatula until just combined — do not overmix.
  6. Fold in the chopped nuts and chocolate chips, if using, with a few gentle strokes.
  7. Divide the batter evenly among the three prepared loaf pans. Give each pan a gentle tap on the counter to settle the batter.
  8. Bake at 325°F for 60 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted in the center of each loaf comes out clean. If the tops are browning too quickly, tent loosely with foil during the last 15 minutes. Allow the loaves to cool in the pans for 10 minutes before turning out onto a wire rack to cool completely.

Tips

  • Don't Squeeze the Zucchini Unlike some recipes, this one benefits from leaving the moisture in the grated zucchini. That liquid is what keeps the bread incredibly moist. Grate it and use it straight — no towel-drying needed.
  • Check Early, Every Oven is Different Start checking the loaves at 50 minutes. Ovens vary, and an overbaked zucchini bread loses its signature tender crumb. A toothpick should come out with just a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.
  • Freeze for Later This recipe makes three loaves — perfect for sharing or freezing. Wrap cooled loaves tightly in plastic wrap, then foil, and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight at room temperature.
  • Toast Your Nuts For deeper flavor, toast the chopped walnuts or pecans in a dry skillet over medium heat for 3–4 minutes before adding them to the batter. It makes a noticeable difference in the finished loaf.
  • Make It Two Loaves Instead If you only have two larger (9×5-inch) loaf pans, divide the batter between them and increase the bake time to approximately 65–70 minutes, checking with a toothpick.
